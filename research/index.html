ABSTRACT

SATWAR simulates a gray-zone confrontation in low Jupiter orbit in 2041: an uninvited inspector shadows an allied radar satellite, and the player has to make it leave without debris or open war. This edition sets out every physical model the game uses, with its equation, its source, how sure we can be of it, and which numbers are design values chosen for play. The game's own code is checked against JPL Horizons, hapsira, scipy, astropy, filterpy, ITU-R recommendations and published worked examples: 762 comparisons across 63 tests, all within tolerance, and 4 further rows that report known simplifications. Section 11 lists what could not be verified.

1. Method and labels

The rule is that every number is either sourced or labelled. Each source claim carries one of four labels:

  • V-TEXT The document was fetched and the passage read; the section, table or page is given.
  • V-RECORD The work exists (publisher, DOI or official listing) but the passage was not read, because of a paywall or because it is a book. The equation used is the standard textbook form.
  • UNVERIFIED Not confirmed. Treat it as unsourced.
  • DERIVED Computed from verified inputs, with the method stated so it can be rerun.

Values chosen for play carry DESIGN VALUE. A design value is never presented as measured. Where possible, it is chosen inside a range that a source does support. The most important design values are the J/S needed to break a link (6 dB), the three hours of lost contact after which the inspector withdraws, the escalation costs, and the compressed timescale of the scenario.

The game runs in Godot 4.7 (GDScript, with 64-bit arithmetic wherever precision matters). Its physics is tested by an external suite (§10), which reads the game's own outputs. Python never reimplements the game in order to grade it.

2. Frames, time and ephemerides

2.1 Reference frame

The simulation is centred on Jupiter. Its plane is Jupiter's equator, defined by the IAU 2015 pole (WGCCRE63), with x toward Jupiter's ascending node on the ICRF equator. The 3D view uses a floating origin at the camera's focus, so positions keep their precision at any distance. The game's clock is UTC. Leap seconds and TT − UTC are ignored, an error of 69.2 s, or 0.16° of Io's orbit.

2.2 The Galilean moons

Each moon is a planar Kepler ellipse built from JPL's mean elements (JUP365). The mean longitude advances at the observed sidereal period, and the mean anomaly at JPL's tabulated anomalistic period, so periapsis precesses at the difference. An earlier build took the mean motion from Kepler's third law, √(μ/a³). That ignores J2 and the Laplace resonance, and it put Io 114° from its true place at the campaign start. The current errors against JPL Horizons are:

2.3 The Sun and planets

All eight planets come from JPL's approximate Keplerian elements (Standish & Williams61): Table 1 for 1800–2050, and Tables 2a and 2b otherwise. They are converted to Jupiter-centred positions in the game frame and cached on a cubic-Hermite grid every 6 h, which adds under 1 km of error. "Earth" in these tables is the Earth–Moon barycentre, 0.0003° from Earth as seen from 5 AU. GM values come from DE44062; radii from WGCCRE 201563; and the solar radius is the IAU 2015 nominal 695,700 km64.

The verification suite checks every planet against Horizons over 2030–2050 and 2060–2199. As a spot check for this edition, the game's distances from Jupiter at the scenario's start (2041-10-12 16:10 UTC) were compared with Horizons vectors (centre 500@599, geometric):

3. Gravity and trajectory design

3.1 Force model and integration

The acceleration on a craft is Jupiter's point mass, its J2 zonal term, and the four Galilean moons as third bodies (direct minus indirect term). The Sun is always on. Planets are added beyond 3 million km from Jupiter, where their tidal pull starts to matter. Inside that radius it is under 10⁻⁷ of Jupiter's (Montenbruck & Gill67 §3.3, Cowell's method):

a = −μ r/|r|³ + aJ2(r) + Σk μk [ (rk − r)/|rk − r|³ − rk/|rk|³ ]

Jupiter's GM is 126,687,000 km³ s⁻² (NASA fact sheet12; 3.7×10⁻⁶ from JPL's JUP365). J2 is 14,696.514×10⁻⁶ at R = 71,492 km (Juno, Folkner 2017; 3.9×10⁻⁶ from Iess 2018). Integration is RK4 with 60 s substeps near Jupiter. Beyond 3 million km the step adapts to 0.004 × the shortest free-fall time √(d³/μ) to any body (Aarseth68). The coefficient is a DESIGN VALUE. Against DOP853 at rtol 10⁻¹³, the integrator stays within 0.03 m over a day.

3.2 Two-body tools

Kepler propagation uses universal variables (Curtis71 Alg. 3.3–3.4), with Danby's starter near e = 1 and Vallado's starter for long hyperbolic coasts (Vallado72 Alg. 8). Lambert's problem is solved by Curtis Alg. 5.2. An earlier loss of precision in the Stumpff function c(z) as z → 4π² made every transfer over 180° fail. The half-angle form 2 sin²(√z/2)/z fixed it, and all 129 test transfers now agree with Izzo's solver (hapsira) to 1.9×10⁻¹².

3.3 Refining under full gravity

At 1.3 Jupiter radii, J2 bends a 3-hour coast by thousands of kilometres, so a two-body Lambert solution misses its target. Every planned burn is therefore refined by differential-correction shooting through the full-gravity coast (Vallado §7.6). Newton's method starts from a two-body Jacobian and updates it with Broyden's method73. HYDRA's transfer to ORIEL-36 flies at 3.570 km/s, against 3.869 km/s two-body, and arrives 0.012 km from its aim point. Circular orbits are initialised with v² = μ/r + 1.5 μ J2 R²/r³, so they don't oscillate.

3.4 The planner

The planner shows a porkchop plot: departure wait against time of flight, each cell a refined transfer coloured by the Δv to go and the Δv to match on arrival. For a target that circles in under 12 hours, waits run to 24 h in 1.5 h steps and flights from 1 to 6 h. Those grid steps are design values. The cheapest rendezvous with ORIEL-36 at the start is a wait of 18 h and a flight of 3 h: 1.90 km/s planned, 1.83 km/s flown, then 1.38 km/s to match.

3.5 Interplanetary flight

Where a campaign allows it, the planner plans between planets with patched conics (Curtis ch. 8; Vallado ch. 12). A heliocentric Lambert arc runs between the planets. The escape burn is fitted so its hyperbola leaves from the ship's actual position. Arrival is costed as capture into an orbit at 1.5 planet radii, a design value. The plan is then refined under full gravity, which is necessary because the Sun's tide during escape bends the departure by about 0.3 km/s. Spheres of influence follow Laplace, r = a(m/M)2/5 (Curtis §8.2).

The Solar System window shows a minimum-energy (Hohmann) estimate for every body:

t = π √(at³/μ☉), at = (r₁ + r₂)/2 v∞ = √(μ☉/r₁) |√(2r₂/(r₁+r₂)) − 1| Δvleave = √(v∞² + 2μJ/rs) − vs (burn at the ship's radius rs and speed vs)

HYDRA orbits at 107,238 km from Jupiter's centre at 34.54 km/s, where the local escape speed is 48.6 km/s. Leaving Jupiter for Earth therefore costs about 14.4 km/s, plus 6.3 km/s to be captured at Earth, against HYDRA's 8.66 km/s. Interplanetary flight is switched off in the first scenario, and these figures show it would be out of reach anyway. That is real physics, not a game limit. From Jupiter's orbit, the cheapest flight takes 3.2 years to Mars and 2.9 years to Earth, and a fall into the Sun takes 2.2 years.

4. Radio: antennas, links and detection

4.1 Antenna gain and patterns

The peak gain of a dish of diameter D at wavelength λ is G₀ = η(πD/λ)², with η = 0.55 throughout. That is 1.24 dB conservative against the DSN 34 m antenna at X band. The beamwidth is about 70λ/D degrees (NAWCWD TP 8347 §3-17; ITU-R SA.509-3 §1.31) V-TEXT.

Off-axis gain follows the ITU reference envelopes: SA.509-3 for large dishes (D/λ ≥ 100), and S.465-5 Note 4 for small ones2:

G(φ) = 52 − 10 log(D/λ) − 25 log φ dBi, 100λ/D ≤ φ < 48° G(φ) = 10 − 10 log(D/λ) dBi, 48° ≤ φ ≤ 180°

These are envelopes of sidelobe peaks, used in interference coordination, so they are conservative for a well-designed antenna. Note 2 of S.465 warns against using them below D/λ = 50, and every Red dish in the game is that small. They are still the most credible public pattern. Every emitter points: Red's telemetry and command antennas at their handler, civil probes at Earth, and the ore hauler at Ganymede. Warships and buoys listen through four-face arrays that form beams in all directions (after the TDRS S-band multiple-access array, NASA TM-105422). Their gain is the face's peak plus 10 log cos(scan). Ganymede Station's 12 m dish has to be pointed: an assist cues it to the most relevant track, and the player can override it.

4.2 Link budget and noise

Pr = Pt Gt(θe) Gr(θr) (λ / 4πR)² (Friis 1946) N = k Ts B

Free-space loss matches ITU-R P.525-4 to 0.0022 dB. That residual is ITU's rounded 32.45 dB constant.

4.3 Detection of unknown bursts

Listeners don't know a burst's waveform, so they detect it with an energy (radiometer) detector (Urkowitz 196729). The integrated SNR grows as √(Bτ). The chance of detection is:

Pd = Q( (Q⁻¹(Pfa) − d) / √(1 + 2s) ), Pfa = 10⁻⁶

The false-alarm rate is a DESIGN VALUE. This expression matches the exact chi-square distributions to 0.01 for n ≥ 10⁵ samples, and even odds come at 6.8 dB. Spread-spectrum (LPI) emitters cost an eavesdropper √(τ/B) in SNR, from the processing gain of ITU-R SM.10555.

4.4 Direction finding and identification

A two-element interferometer measures a bearing with

σθ = λ/(2πd) · σφ, σφ² = (1 + 2s) / (2Bτ s²)

This is the cross-correlation phase error for a noise-like signal (Knapp & Carter 1976), checked by a Monte Carlo of a two-element correlator to 3%. A 20 µrad floor is a DESIGN VALUE. Bearings from separated listeners are crossed by weighted least squares. Identification compares a recording's modulation and symbol rate against a threat library above a minimum SNR, a design-value gate consistent with specific-emitter identification needing high-SNR recordings (Danev et al.35). Once a signal is identified, the coherent turnaround ratio fup = fdown × 749/880 (CCSDS 401; DSN 810-0056) gives its command uplink.

5. Electromagnetic attack

5.1 The jamming equation

Jamming a command uplink means being louder, at the victim's receiver, than its handler. Both paths are one-way, so the equation is Friis applied twice. This is the communications form of NAWCWD TP 8347 §4-9 V-TEXT, and Adamy's EW 10148 gives it in dB form:

J = Pj + Gj(aim error) + Gv(θj) − Lj S = Pu + Gu + Gv(0) − Ls J/S = J − S − 10 log(Bj/Bv) dB

Gv(θj) is the victim's gain toward the jammer, at angle θj off its line to the handler. From broadside, the victim's discrimination is −44.8 dB. Near the handler's line it falls to −13 dB, so geometry matters. The aim term means a loose track costs beam-pointing gain. HYDRA's jammer is 1 kW from four combined 250 W TWTAs into a 1.5 m dish at 7.18 GHz, 68.5 dBW EIRP. That is a plausible descendant of the Counter Communications System. Real CCS and Meadowlands power figures are not public, so this is an extrapolation DESIGN VALUE.

5.2 Spot and barrage

Before the signal is identified, the jammer must spread its power across the whole deep-space X uplink allocation, 7145–7190 MHz (DSN 810-005 Module 201 Rev. F V-TEXT). That costs 10 log(45) = 16.53 dB against a 1 MHz channel. An earlier build used 90 MHz, which included the near-Earth 7190–7235 MHz allocation. Spot jamming of the identified channel removes the penalty. Measured from beside the inspector, barrage gives:

6. Heat, infrared and visible signatures

6.1 Energy balance

Each craft has a skin and, if it carries a reactor, radiators. Skin temperature follows a single-node balance with heat capacity (Gilmore69 ch. 2):

C dT/dt = Pleak + α S f A/4 − ε σ T⁴ A, C = 897 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ × 0.25 × mdry

S = 50.26 W m⁻² at Jupiter (NASA fact sheet V-TEXT), and f is the sunlit share (§6.4). The heat capacity is aluminium's (Incropera70), and the 25% skin fraction is a DESIGN VALUE. HYDRA's thermal time constant is about 22.5 h, so a pass through Jupiter's shadow cools it only from 190 K to about 187 K. Absorptance and emittance come from NASA RP-1121 (Henninger 198413).

Reactor ships use JIMO's 100 kWe Brayton plant at 20.2% efficiency (Mason 200314). That rejects 395 kW through two-sided Lambertian radiators, I(θ) = εσT⁴A cos θ/π. WARDEN's 85 m² per face runs at 462 K, within 1.1% of JIMO's design point (364 kWt from 170 m², Siamidis 200515). Radiators lie flat to the plane of play, so every observer in the plane sees only their edge.

6.2 Going dark

A ship can stop radiating for a while by storing its heat. WARDEN boils off and vents 2 t of water (2.5 MJ/kg), which carries 395 kW for 3.5 h and is then gone. VESPER melts 500 kg of ice (333 kJ/kg, Leimkuehler 201119) for 15.4 h and refreezes it afterwards.

6.3 In-band infrared detection

Telescopes work in NEO Surveyor's NC2 band, 6–10 µm (Mainzer et al. 202340 V-TEXT). The in-band share of each body's emission comes from the Widger & Woodall series, which matches astropy to 6×10⁻¹¹. The 0.5 m LWIR's 1σ noise-equivalent irradiance is 7×10⁻¹⁸ W m⁻². That lies inside the 0.4–1.1×10⁻¹⁷ range derived from NEO Surveyor's S/N-5 sensitivity. A look detects with

S/N = I / R² / (NEI × background), Pd = Φ(S/N − 5)

Against Jupiter's disc, the background raises the NEI ×50, a DESIGN VALUE inside the ×26–92 derived for a background-limited sensor. Within 3 Jupiter radii off the disc, stray light raises it ×5, also a design value. False alarms are Poisson, with a mean of 0.1 per sensor per look (design value), and only lines from two places at once start a track.

6.4 Shadows and reflected light

The Sun is a limb-darkened disc, I(μ) = 1 − u(1 − μ) with u = 0.6 (Allen's66 §14.7), sampled by 397 rays in 12 rings. The sunlit share is the weighted fraction not blocked by oblate Jupiter (71,492 × 66,854 km), the four moons, or, far out, a planet. This gives both umbra and penumbra (Montenbruck & Gill §3.4.2), within 0.019 of a dense numerical integration.

Sunlit hulls are also seen in visible light. The Sun's MV = 4.81 (Willmer 2018) is scaled to Jupiter's irradiance, and each hull is a Lambert sphere with phase law (sin α + (π − α) cos α)/π. A 1 m GEODSS-class telescope reaches magnitude 22 at S/N 5 (Stokes et al. 1996). Visible telescopes look only where a track already exists, because reaching magnitude 22 takes about 100 s staring at one field.

7. Tracking

Every contact is a track: an estimate with a covariance, not ground truth. Bearings update it through an unscented Kalman filter (Julier sigma points, κ = 0, circular mean for angles), which matches filterpy to 3×10⁻¹². Prediction uses central-difference state transition matrices and white-noise acceleration (Bar-Shalom et al. 2001). Two cases needed special handling.

  • Close in. With HYDRA a few kilometres from the inspector, sigma points can straddle the sensor. Within a tenth of the range, a bearing therefore enters as a pseudo-linear measurement (Lingren & Gong 1978; Aidala & Nardone 1982). Between craft in nearly the same orbit, range is unobservable from angles alone without a manoeuvre (Woffinden & Geller 2009). So without a recent radar range, the track keeps at least 0.5 × range of 1σ along the line of sight (design value). In 42 looks from alongside to departure, no estimate fell outside 3σ.
  • Unseen burns. A bearing whose normalised innovation passes the χ²(1) 99.9% point (10.83) is taken as a burn nobody saw, and widens the track (Bar-Shalom ch. 11). The 50 m/s and 10 min used are design values.

A track nobody has detected for 24 h, or whose 1σ exceeds 100,000 km, is dropped (after Blackman & Popoli 1999). Both thresholds are design values. Contacts are drawn after MIL-STD-2525D, with a dashed frame for a last known position.

8. Doctrine and the kill chain

The game's verbs follow US joint doctrine, read from the published texts: JP 3-85 Joint Electromagnetic Spectrum Operations (2020)10, JP 3-60 Joint Targeting (2018)9, AFDP 3-60 (2026)11 and the USSF Space Warfighting framework (2025)8.

Under JP 3-85, a stealth ship going dark is practising electromagnetic protection: "multispectral low-observability" is one of its listed examples. Doctrine says nothing about TDOA/FDOA, look-through, own-platform jammer isolation, or an escalation ladder. It also never uses the words "spoofing" or "reversible": its terms are intrusion and deception, and temporary or nonlethal effects. The escalation meter is therefore entirely a design value. Its ordering (reversible and deniable acts cheap, debris-generating acts ruinous) follows doctrine's escalation-of-force guidance qualitatively.

9. The scenario and its real-world basis

THE SHADOWING is modelled on a real uncooperative rendezvous and proximity operation against a commercial X-band radar-imaging satellite in October 2026. It was reported from LeoLabs tracking data by Breaking Defense and The War Zone on 19 October 202675,76. The events are moved to Jupiter and the craft are fictional. As reported, the real sequence was: coplanar alignment; closing from hundreds of kilometres; station-keeping within 5 km, then 1–2 km; a closest pass reported at 500 m (another report gives 26 m); moves ahead of and behind the target; and a controlled departure into a larger orbit. The inspector's seven moves follow that sequence: −60, −5, +5, −1.5, −0.5 (the long loiter), +2 and −0.5 km along-track.

  • Orbit. 1.3 RJ (92,940 km), a 4.37 h period. This is inside Jupiter's radiation belts, so every craft is assumed rad-hardened (Juno's titanium vault cuts its dose about 800 times77). No dose is modelled.
  • The inspector's radio. An X-band (8.436 GHz) BPSK burst at 256 kbaud, 10 W into a 0.4 m dish aimed at its handler, 90 s long, after each move. These are design values within ordinary CCSDS practice. Seeing a threat, it goes quiet for 6 hours.
  • Losing its link. Cut off from its handler for 3 hours within 200 km of its target, it backs away prograde at 4 m/s and departs. This is grounded in practice, not in a published rule: a Progress approach to the ISS was aborted on loss of telemetry in 200878, and ESA's ATV carried RETREAT and ESCAPE modes for the same reason. The threshold and the burn are DESIGN VALUES.
  • Timescale. The real event took weeks. The game compresses it to about 57 hours over three days DESIGN VALUE.

11. Limits, simplifications and open questions

Not public, so extrapolated

  • Jammer, uplink and handler EIRPs. Counter Communications System and Meadowlands power is not published.
  • Weapon-class laser wall-plug efficiency. The game's 25% is conservative against industrial fibre lasers.
  • Time-transfer accuracy between platforms at Jupiter, where there is no GPS.

Not verified

  • Mid-infrared zodiacal background at 5.2 AU, and Jupiter's band-resolved brightness (a grey body at 125.6 K is used).
  • HawkEye 360 geolocation accuracy; the Albersheim validity limits; specific-emitter identification accuracy; look-through duty cycles.
  • The 2024 edition of JP 3-60 (the 2018 edition was read), and JP 5-0's definitions of MOE and MOP.
  • MIL-STD-2525D was read through a reproduction of its tables, not the original PDF.

Simplifications

  • Moons are planar mean-element ellipses with no mutual perturbations (≤ 23,000 km and ≤ 0.72°).
  • Radiators lie flat to the plane, and the effect of jamming is binary (link cut or not). Doctrine describes a continuum from temporary to permanent and from partial to complete.
  • The umpire's view: some kill-chain indicators still read ground truth rather than Blue's assessed belief.
  • Red doesn't yet adapt by frequency hopping, nulling or switching to optical links. All of these are real (ITU-R SM.1055; Cummings 1992; NASA DSOC).
  • No radiation dose is modelled.
